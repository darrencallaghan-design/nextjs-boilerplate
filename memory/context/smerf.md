# SMERF — Full Knowledge Base for Engine Agent Discovery

## What SMERF Is

SMERF = Social, Military, Educational, Religious, Fraternal. These groups book event space, room blocks, and F&B like corporate groups — but the defining difference is **self-funded vs. employer-funded**. SMERF guests pay their own bill. This shapes everything about how they buy.

Extended universe (SMERF+R): recreational, entertainment, sports groups — high school sports teams, hobbyist clubs, car enthusiasts, competitive gaming. Common events: family reunions, weddings, religious seminars, Rotary/Elks meetings.

---

## The Five Segments

### Social (S)
- Weddings, civic/professional orgs, charity events, holiday celebrations, interest groups
- Group size: 15–100+ guests; often multi-event (receptions, ceremonies, banquets)
- **Weddings** = highest-value sub-segment, price-inelastic at event level
- **Family reunions** = volume play — recurring, predictable, loyalty-driven, geography-tied

### Military (M)
- Chain hotels preferred for discounted room blocks + familiar amenities nationwide
- **Military reunions** among the most frequent SMERF bookings at large hotels
- Benefits from GSA/government per diem rate programs — rate transparency matters
- Volume drivers: **VFW, American Legion, AMVETS** — national conventions + regional chapter meetings on regular cadence

### Educational (E)
- K–12: academic competitions, sports tournaments, band/choir travel
- Higher ed: **alumni associations, student government, Greek life**
- Professional educators: teacher conferences, district administrator offsites
- **Long, predictable lead times** — follow academic calendars
- Budget sensitivity HIGH — institutional and often grant-funded
- Needs: large meeting spaces, tech-equipped conference rooms

### Religious (R)
- Church retreats, youth group trips, diocesan conferences, denominational conventions
- **Most recession-proof sub-segment** — mission-driven, happens regardless of economy
- Volume drivers: **Southern Baptist Convention, COGIC, LDS church groups** — move thousands of rooms at a time
- Needs: spaces for reflection/prayer, dietary restriction respect, serene atmosphere

### Fraternal (F)
- **Lions Clubs, Rotary International**, local business chapters — formal meeting spaces + banquet facilities
- Long-term, recurring booking patterns with preferred venues
- **Greek-letter organizations** (fraternities, sororities)
- **BGLOs (historically Black Greek-letter organizations)** — convention travel rivals mid-sized corporate meetings in room block volume
- Service organizations, professional societies, honor societies

## Why Hotels Care About SMERF

### Occupancy Timing
- Corporate travel dominates Mon–Thu; **SMERF fills Thu–Sun**
- Structurally essential for full-week RevPAR optimization

### Off-Season Revenue
- Consistent revenue during off-peak seasons; mission-driven gatherings don't cancel

### Recession Resilience
- High-end tourism and corporate travel decline in downturns; SMERF continues
- Purpose-based, community-supported gatherings are not discretionary

### Post-COVID Recovery
- SMERF has been a leading demand-recovery segment post-COVID
- "As meetings become more regionalized, we continue to see bookings on a shorter-term basis within 12–18 months" (Hilton SF Union Square)

### Best Hotel Type Fit
- **Economy/midscale**: SMERF is foundational revenue, especially off-season
- **Upscale/luxury**: secondary unless occupancy needs filling
- **Urban/convention center**: corporate first, SMERF for weekend boosts
- **Suburban/secondary-market**: may rely on SMERF as core driver

## How SMERF Groups Actually Buy

### Who Does the Booking
- Often **volunteers or inexperienced members** — not professional travel managers
- A PTA parent, church administrator, fraternity chapter president
- **Inexperienced, time-poor, deeply anxious about making a mistake**
- Require guidance and patience on contracts, BEOs, payment schedules

### How They Find Hotels
- Start on **Google**, rely on word of mouth, return to same venue once trust is built
- **CVB (Convention & Visitors Bureau)** network is a meaningful starting channel
- More likely to book without agencies vs. corporate travel

### What They Prioritize
1. **Price and value** — above all
2. **Ease of booking**
3. **Dedicated point of contact** who holds their hand
4. Want to feel optimistic and comfortable; reassurance matters

### Lead Times
- Annual fraternal/religious conventions: **12–18+ months out**
- Ad hoc social groups (family reunions): shorter

## Where Engine Fits: The ICP

Engine's ICP within SMERF is **not the individual SMERF traveler** — it's the **organization that manages or coordinates travel for SMERF-adjacent members on a recurring basis**.

### Structural ICP Signal (use this to qualify targets)
An org is a strong Engine fit when ALL of these are true:
- **10–500 employees or members who travel regularly**
- **A single person or small team** responsible for booking/coordination (not a full procurement function)
- Travel budget that's real but **not large enough to justify a full TMC relationship**
- Strong preference for cost control and transparency
- High likelihood of repeat bookings once trust is established
- **No existing corporate travel tool**, or using something inadequate (personal credit cards, Expedia, direct hotel calls)

### Highest-Fit SMERF ICP Profiles (ranked)

**1. Associations & Membership Organizations** ← HIGHEST PRIORITY
- National and regional associations with field staff or member travel
- Trade associations, professional societies, alumni associations
- Staff who travel individually + coordinate group gatherings
- Underserved by TMCs (too small/non-corporate) + overwhelmed by consumer OTAs
- **This is the sweet spot**

**2. Religious Denominations & Large Church Networks**
- Multi-campus churches, denominational administrative offices, faith-based nonprofits
- Admin staff travel for ministry + coordinate retreats/conferences
- Decision-maker: office manager, church administrator, executive pastor — NOT a travel manager
- Engine's simplicity is a perfect fit

**3. Educational Institutions (Non-Higher Ed)**
- School districts coordinating sports travel, academic competition travel, admin staff travel
- Budget-conscious, no dedicated travel desk, needs rate assurance + compliance
- Charter schools and private K–12 with lean admin teams are particularly interesting

**4. Fraternal & Service Organizations with National Infrastructure**
- Elks, VFW, Knights of Columbus, BGLOs — national conventions + chapter-level travel
- Chapter administrators or national event coordinators are the Engine buyer
- Recurring conventions + strong brand identity = strong loyalty once converted

**5. Military Support Organizations & Veteran Groups**
- TAPS, Blue Star Families, major VSOs (Veteran Service Organizations)
- Staff travel + group event coordination
- Benefit from government/non-profit rates; admin staff managing logistics

## Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Why They Don't Serve SMERF Well |
|------------|----------------------------------|
| **Cvent** | Dominates large corporate meetings; RFP process designed for professional planners, not volunteers. Overbuilt and overpriced for true SMERF. |
| **HelmsBriscoe / ConferenceDirect** | Intermediaries, commission model, focus on larger groups. |
| **CVBs** | Free channel but slow, manual, not platform-native. |
| **Direct to hotel** | Still dominant for small groups but no data trail, no rate assurance, no org-level control. |
| **OTAs (Expedia, Booking.com)** | Used by individuals but can't handle block bookings; no organizational-level oversight. |

**Engine's differentiation**: Platform gives the organization visibility and control at rates equivalent to or better than direct — genuinely differentiated for the mid-market SMERF tier.

### Qualifying Questions for Each Discovered Org
- Does this org have a national headquarters with paid staff?
- Do members/staff travel for chapter meetings, conventions, or programs?
- Is there a single coordinator who manages travel logistics?
- Does the org have 10–500 traveling members (not too small, not TMC-sized)?
- Is there no obvious existing corporate travel tool?

### Target Titles at Each Org
Executive Director, CEO, President, COO, VP of Programs, Director of Events, Director of Operations, Chief of Staff, National Administrator, Executive Vice President

- Self-funded buyers care intensely about rate → Engine's negotiated rates = direct financial benefit

- Recurring booking patterns = long-term partnership for both sides
